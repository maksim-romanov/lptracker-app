# Keep scroll native, fake the premium

The 2025-2026 consensus is "scroll-linked, not scroll-owned": a tall track with a `position: sticky` stage, native scroll left alone, and progress mapped to visuals. The prototype's architecture (sticky stage, rAF progress, Canvas 2D, CSS frames, proximity snap, `inert`, static reduced-motion fallback) already matches that consensus. Three problems need action. First, **Apple's Marketing Guidelines prohibit "any simulation of an Apple product"**, so hand-built CSS iPhone, iPad and Mac frames carry legal risk, and the "realism" effects (glare, shadow, tilt) are the most exposed part. Second, **`inert` on hidden scenes removes their text from find-in-page and the accessibility tree**, so all scene copy needs a second, always-readable home. Third, mobile needs explicit work: `svh` heights, no resize on toolbar changes, a particle cap, and a frame-time governor, because iOS Low Power Mode caps rAF at 30 fps. Canvas 2D is defensible at a few hundred to roughly 2,000 sprites, but only with pre-rendered glow sprites and no `shadowBlur`. The performance sources are firm on qualitative rules and thin on numbers, and the sourcing for particle craft (depth, links, dissolve) is weakest of all. The prioritized list follows, then a section on what to change in your build.

## Prioritized recommendations at a glance

| # | Recommendation | Support |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Resolve the Apple device-frame question before shipping: use unmodified official bezels flat and static, or a deliberately non-Apple abstract frame | Strong on the rules, ambiguous on scope and enforcement |
| 2 | Keep all scene copy in DOM reading order; treat `inert` as a trade-off and keep one always-visible text layer | Strong (MDN, W3C) |
| 3 | Build a real reduced-motion path: unpinned stacked scenes, frozen canvas frame, no ambient loop, plus a visible toggle | Strong (W3C, WebKit, web.dev) |
| 4 | Use `svh` for scene height, avoid canvas resize on toolbar-driven height change, keep touch scrolling native | Strong (MDN, GSAP) |
| 5 | Animate only `transform` and `opacity`; pre-bake glows; no animated blur or `backdrop-filter` over the canvas | Strong on principle, weak on numbers |
| 6 | Pre-render glow sprites, draw additively, cap DPR and particle count, add a frame-time governor | MDN-backed for sprites and DPR scaling; thresholds are heuristics |
| 7 | Pause the loop when the scene is off-screen or the tab is hidden; handle iOS Low Power Mode by cadence | Partly sourced |
| 8 | Keep `scroll-snap` at `proximity`; never `mandatory` on tall scenes | Medium (MDN warning plus inference) |
| 9 | Add grain over the flat colour to prevent banding | Medium |
| 10 | Make the finale illustrative: abstract labels, no real-looking numbers | Repo rule plus inference |

## Native sticky scroll is the defensible base, and snap is the risk

A tall section holding a viewport-sized stage, with scroll progress mapped to animation, is the lowest-risk pattern. Apple-style pages use roughly **400vh per sequence** with a canvas held in view, though that figure comes from a third-party write-up rather than Apple ([SetProduct](https://www.setproduct.com/blog/how-to-3d-animate-scroll)). GSAP's ScrollTrigger is the JS equivalent, but it adds spacer management, and pins break under ancestors with `transform` or `will-change` ([GSAP docs](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/ScrollTrigger/)). Your plain-sticky choice avoids that class of bug. The criticism of scroll-jacking (overriding browser behaviour, breaking assistive tech, triggering vestibular symptoms) is repeated across secondary articles ([Web Designer Depot](https://webdesignerdepot.com/how-scrolljacking-breaks-ux-fundamentals/), [SitePoint](https://www.sitepoint.com/scrolljacking-accessibility/)), and no Nielsen Norman source was read, so treat it as consensus rather than measurement.

Scroll-snap is where native pages go wrong. MDN states that `mandatory` forces a snap point after scrolling ends while `proximity` snaps only when near one, that snapping can interfere with keyboard navigation, and that items taller than the viewport cause snapping problems ([MDN scroll snap](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/CSS_scroll_snap)). Scenes that are 200-300vh tall are exactly that case. Staying on `proximity` is the right call. No primary source covers Page Down and Space behaviour under snap with oversized scenes, so test it in Safari, Chrome and Firefox rather than assuming.

Smoothing is optional and a trap. Lenis keeps native scroll, so sticky still works, but it does not work with CSS scroll-snap (it ships a separate snap plugin), is capped at 60 fps in Safari and 30 fps in low-power mode, and respects reduced motion by default ([Lenis README](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis)). Since you use CSS snap, skip Lenis. A small lerp on the progress value inside your rAF loop is equivalent in effect to Lenis `lerp` or GSAP `scrub: n`; the notes found no sourced constant for it, and stacking two smoothers compounds lag. Native CSS `animation-timeline` runs off the main thread, but the sources conflict on browser support. The WebKit blog places it in Safari 26 and Chrome has it from 115, while Firefox is still not Baseline per MDN ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/17101/a-guide-to-scroll-driven-animations-with-just-css/), [Chrome](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/css-ui/scroll-driven-animations), [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/animation-timeline)). Keep JS as the baseline and use CSS timelines only as `@supports` enhancement for simple effects. One search summary claiming earlier Safari and Firefox shipping contradicts these sources and should be ignored.

No source gives authoritative scene lengths or hold times. The heuristic in the notes (about 150-300vh per scene, 15-25% enter, 50-70% hold, 15-25% exit, text held 2-3 seconds) is unsourced; measure a few reference sites in DevTools (spacer height divided by viewport height) and tune by testing.

## Performance: stay on the compositor and spend the budget on glow sprites

The firmest rules come from web.dev and MDN. Restrict animation to `transform` and `opacity` so it stays on the compositing stage; in web.dev's comparison, `transform` cut dropped frames from about 50% to about 1% against `top`/`left` ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide)). Blurs and shadows are slow to paint, so pre-bake them. `will-change` is a last resort and excessive use causes memory pressure; MDN suggests setting it just before a change and clearing it after ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/will-change)). Keep it to a few long-lived layers, not every reveal element. Claims that `backdrop-filter` cost scales with area and radius, and that radius should stay under 10-20 px, come from a secondary blog with no measurements ([OpenReplay](https://blog.openreplay.com/creating-blurred-backgrounds-css-backdrop-filter/)); the direction is credible, the numbers are weak. The practical rule is to avoid a blurred element sitting over a canvas that changes every frame.

For Canvas 2D, MDN is explicit: pre-render repeated objects to an offscreen canvas and `drawImage` them, avoid `shadowBlur`, use integer coordinates, do not scale images inside `drawImage`, batch calls, and use `getContext("2d", {alpha:false})` where the canvas is opaque ([MDN canvas optimization](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Canvas_API/Tutorial/Optimizing_canvas)). Pairing a pre-rendered radial-gradient sprite with `globalCompositeOperation = 'lighter'` is the standard bloom substitute, but no measured benchmark supports a particle ceiling for Canvas 2D on mobile. The notes' judgement is that WebGL with one instanced draw is the safe default for 2-5k additive particles ([MDN WebGL best practices](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/WebGL_API/WebGL_best_practices), [WebGL Fundamentals](https://webglfundamentals.org/webgl/lessons/webgl-instanced-drawing.html)), and OGL (about 29 kB minzip) is far lighter than three.js ([OGL](https://github.com/oframe/ogl)). A few hundred to about 2,000 sprites in Canvas 2D is plausible, so stay there and do not migrate unless profiling on a mid-range phone fails. If you draw constellation links, bucket particles in a grid with cell size equal to the link distance and check only neighbouring cells to avoid O(n^2); this is a standard algorithm with no web-specific source.

Rendering at lower resolution is endorsed: MDN suggests a back buffer smaller than CSS size (its example is 0.75x) as an explicit quality-for-speed trade. The common `min(devicePixelRatio, 1.5-2)` cap is community convention without a primary source, and soft glows tolerate a low cap. A frame-time governor (rolling average of rAF deltas, step down DPR, then count, then links, with hysteresis) is conventional but has no authoritative reference, so its thresholds (the notes suggest about 20 ms sustained) are heuristic. OffscreenCanvas with a worker is Baseline since March 2023 ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/OffscreenCanvas)) and mainly pays off for CPU-bound simulation, which a Canvas 2D finale is; treat it as a second step after the simpler fixes.

Pausing matters on phones. Browsers stop rAF in background tabs ([MDN Page Visibility](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Page_Visibility_API)), but a scene scrolled out of view in a visible tab keeps drawing unless you stop it, so gate the loop with an IntersectionObserver (this specific pattern is inference, not sourced). iOS Low Power Mode throttles rAF to 30 fps by design from iOS 14 ([WebKit bug 215745](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=215745), [Motion](https://motion.dev/magazine/when-browsers-throttle-requestanimationframe)). There is no API to detect it, so measure cadence and make all motion time-based (delta-time), not frame-based, so the finale does not run at half speed.

Core Web Vitals targets are LCP at or below 2.5 s, INP at or below 200 ms and CLS at or below 0.1 at the 75th percentile ([web.dev Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)). A canvas is not on web.dev's list of LCP candidates, so the LCP element should be headline text or a sized poster image; start the particle loop after first paint. Reserve space for the canvas with `aspect-ratio` or fixed height, and use `size-adjust` fallbacks for fonts ([web.dev CLS](https://web.dev/articles/optimize-cls)). For measurement, Long Animation Frames (Chromium 123+, absent in Firefox and Safari) attribute slow frames to scripts ([Chrome](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/long-animation-frames)). Image sequences are the Apple pattern for photoreal 3D, but they cost tens of megabytes (the AirPods example runs 50 MB or more) and are a poor fit for abstract UI scenes ([SetProduct](https://www.setproduct.com/blog/how-to-3d-animate-scroll)); live DOM and canvas are right for your case.

## Particle craft is thinly sourced; use restraint and fake the depth

Honest summary: primary sources on constellation, link and dissolve techniques were not found, and the notes mark them as untested synthesis. What is supported is narrow. Depth of field in particle scenes is faked by making blur radius a function of distance from a focal plane ([Codrops, via search summary; page returned 403](https://tympanus.net/codrops/?p=43442)), which reduces on Canvas 2D to size and alpha falling off with depth plus a few large, soft near-camera discs (inference). Bloom through three.js `UnrealBloomPass` does not composite correctly over HTML on a transparent canvas, according to a forum answer rather than maintainer documentation ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/unrealbloompass-makes-background-black/38994)). That favours additive soft sprites on a transparent canvas, which is your approach anyway.

Additive blending brightens toward white, so check it against your single brand colour. If the page is dark, `lighter` works. If the background is light or saturated, additive glow washes out and you need normal alpha blending with a tinted sprite (inference, no source). Banding on large flat or dark gradients is an 8-bit quantisation problem that extra gradient stops do not fix; a faint noise layer does, commonly via SVG `feTurbulence` with `fractalNoise` and `numOctaves` of 3-4 ([master.dev](https://blog.master.dev/grainy-gradients/)). The notes suggest a pre-rendered tiled noise PNG at low opacity is cheaper than a live SVG filter over a full-screen element; that is judgement, and no sourced opacity values exist.

On motion timing, sources agree on ease-out for entering elements and strong custom curves over the weak built-ins, with UI motion under about 300 ms ([Emil Kowalski](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/7-practical-animation-tips)), and Apple's HIG says not to add motion for its own sake and not to make motion the only carrier of information ([Apple HIG Motion](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion.json)). The sub-300 ms rule targets repeated UI interactions; one-time story reveals commonly run 0.6-1.0 s (inference). Scrubbed motion should be smoothed rather than eased, because scroll position already is the timeline (practice, unsourced). A restraint rule of one primary moving element per beat is design heuristic only. No primary source covers how Apple, Linear or Stripe time their transitions, and the Linear figures in the notes come from a third-party summary, so do not cite them.

## Device mockups: Apple's rules cut against realism

Apple's Marketing Guidelines say to use product images "as is," with modifications defined as adding reflections, shadows or highlights, cropping, tilting or obstructing, and "animating, flipping, or spinning the images." They also list as unauthorized "rendering in 3D or creating any simulation of an Apple product" and "illustrations that depict an Apple product" ([Apple Marketing Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/marketing/guidelines/)). Promotional copy may sit beside product images, not on top of them. Minimum onscreen device size is 200 px, and relative product scale must be correct when several devices appear (same page). The official bezels are downloadable from the [Design Resources](https://developer.apple.com/design/resources/) page, which defers legal terms to those guidelines.

Read literally, a CSS-built iPhone with a Dynamic Island is a "simulation," and glare, shadows and scroll-driven tilt on it are the modifications the rules prohibit for official art. Two points stay unresolved. The text is framed around promoting apps, so whether it binds your own site is not stated, and no source shows enforcement against CSS lookalikes or how Linear, Raycast or Arc handle it. The notes are not legal advice. A vendor's claim that "generic shapes drawn in CSS" avoid the issue ([react-mockframe](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/react-mockframe@1.1.0/README.md)) is marketing, not a determination, and trade-dress claims over the rounded-corner black-front design come only from a secondary summary ([Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20110420_iphone_galaxy)). The decision is the owner's.

The safe options are three. Use the official bezel PNG unmodified, flat, front-on, with only the screen content animating. Use a deliberately abstract frame that does not copy the Dynamic Island, camera or edge details. Or show screenshots with no frame. For accuracy in a generic frame, use confirmed specs: iPhone 17 is 2622x1206 (screen ratio about 0.46), the 13-inch iPad Pro 2752x2064 (3:4), and the 14-inch MacBook Pro 3024x1964 with a top notch ([iPhone compare](https://www.apple.com/iphone/compare/), [iPad Pro](https://www.apple.com/ipad-pro/specs/), [MacBook Pro](https://www.apple.com/macbook-pro/specs/)). Corner radii, island dimensions and bezel widths are not published, so measure them from the bezel files; bezel widths in the notes (about 2.4 mm iPhone, 8 mm iPad) are derived estimates. Relative widths of roughly 2.83 : 8.48 : 12.31 inches (iPhone : iPad : MacBook Pro 14) satisfy the scale rule. Download badges have their own rules: unmodified, not angled or animated, one App Store badge per layout and subordinate to the main message, 1/4-height clear space, 40 px minimum height ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/marketing/guidelines/)). Google's guidance requires equal or larger size for the Play badge when marketplaces sit together ([Android developers](https://developer.android.google.cn/distribute/tools/promote/badges)); the mirror in the notes is old, so check the live page.

## Accessibility and mobile: the rules that bite pinned pages

WCAG 2.3.3 (AAA) is the criterion that matters: motion triggered by interaction must be disableable, and its intent text names scroll-moved elements as vestibular triggers; `prefers-reduced-motion` is a listed technique ([W3C 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)). WCAG 2.2.2 applies to any ambient loop that auto-starts and runs over 5 seconds ([W3C 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)), so an idle twinkling star field needs a pause control or must be off under reduced motion. WCAG 2.3.1 limits flashes to three per second, relevant to bright scene cuts and bloom ([W3C 2.3.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html)). WebKit advises reducing selectively: scaling, zooming, spinning and parallax are the vestibular triggers, while small progress indicators are low-risk ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/7551/responsive-design-for-motion/), [WebKit scroll-driven guide](https://webkit.org/blog/17101/a-guide-to-scroll-driven-animations-with-just-css/)). Because desktop users often leave the OS setting off, a visible on-page toggle is cheap insurance (inference).

`inert` is widely available and removes content from focus, find-in-page and the accessibility tree ([MDN inert](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Reference/Global_attributes/inert)). That is correct for hidden controls but means searching for a word from scene 2 while on scene 1 finds nothing. Alternatives: `content-visibility: auto` keeps content findable while skipping offscreen rendering and needs `contain-intrinsic-size` ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Properties/content-visibility)), and `hidden="until-found"` supports find-in-page for collapsed content ([MDN hidden](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Reference/Global_attributes/hidden)). The notes leave the trade-off unresolved by any source. Focus must not be obscured by sticky layers (WCAG 2.4.11), fixed with `scroll-padding` ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-not-obscured-minimum.html)), and 100vh pinned scenes with text clip at 320 px reflow, so drop the pin at small sizes ([W3C 1.4.10](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html)). In forced-colors mode shadows and background images are removed, so glow-only focus rings and gradient text fail ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/At-rules/@media/forced-colors)). Treat the canvas as decorative with `aria-hidden` (standard practice, no primary source retrieved), and check text contrast against the worst-case frame. Split-text headlines are a measured hazard: Adrian Roselli found GSAP SplitText worked in only 2 of 8 screen reader and browser combinations and recommends not splitting words into letters ([Roselli, Feb 2026](https://adrianroselli.com/2026/02/you-know-what-just-dont-split-words-into-letters.html)).

On mobile, `dvh` "can cause layout shifts during scrolling," and MDN advises using `svh` or `lvh` explicitly rather than `dvh` for large areas ([MDN length](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Values/length)). GSAP documents that address-bar show/hide resizes cause recalculation jumps and offers `normalizeScroll` and `ignoreMobileResize` as opt-in fixes ([GSAP](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/ScrollTrigger/static.normalizeScroll())), which confirms that touch scrolling should be left native. iOS canvases have memory limits: 16,777,216 px maximum area (4096x4096, 64 MB RGBA) and a total-memory warning reported near 384 MB on iOS 15 ([PQINA](https://www.pqina.nl/blog/total-canvas-memory-use-exceeds-the-maximum-limit/), [PQINA area](https://pqina.nl/blog/canvas-area-exceeds-the-maximum-limit/)); these figures are from third-party and forum sources, and version-specific. A single report that resizing an on-screen WebGL canvas leaks memory on iOS ([Apple forum](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/668999)) is weak, but it supports resizing only on width changes.

Claims with weak or no support, collected: the 30 fps Low Power Mode behaviour is sourced, but detecting it by cadence is unsourced; scene length and hold-time ratios, DPR caps, governor thresholds, glow behaviour on light backgrounds, link and pulse techniques, grain opacity and shadow softness have no source; `backdrop-filter` numeric thresholds, the three.js bundle size (a dated forum thread) and "8.5x" WebGPU gains (a blog) should not be repeated; Safari 26 GA status for scroll-driven animations is reported as "26 beta" in the source and needs checking; and WCAG 2.5.8 target size (24x24 CSS px) and `prefers-contrast` were not verified from primary sources.

## What to change in our current build

The build is sound in outline. These changes are ordered by priority, with the evidence behind each.

**1. Decide the frame question first (owner decision).** Per [Apple's guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/marketing/guidelines/), CSS-built iPhone, iPad and Mac frames are the highest-risk part of the page, and the realism layer (glare, shadows, scroll-linked tilt) is exactly what the rules prohibit on official art. Options in order of safety: official bezels, flat and static, with only screen content animating; an abstract frame with no Dynamic Island, camera or titanium detail copy; or bare screenshots. If the CSS frames stay, drop the island shape and logo-like details, keep shadows on the page element rather than the device, and record the decision in an ADR. Keep the three devices at correct relative scale and at least 200 px.

**2. Fix `inert` side effects.** Keep `inert` on hidden scenes for tab order and screen readers, but accept that find-in-page skips them; add a visually-present or screen-reader reachable text summary of all three scenes, or test `content-visibility: auto` on non-interactive copy. Add `scroll-padding` for any sticky header, and when focus or an anchor targets a scene, scroll the page to that scene's offset so visuals match focus. Include a skip link and a visible progress indicator with at least 24 px targets.

**3. Harden the reduced-motion path.** The static fallback should be unpinned stacked sections, a frozen finale frame, no ambient loop, and no scale, zoom or parallax. Listen for `change` on the media query so toggling it takes effect live ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/prefers-reduced-motion)), and add an on-page toggle. Also cover `forced-colors` (borders instead of shadows, no gradient-only text), `@media print` (unpin, show all scenes, reset inline transforms), and a small-height or 320 px layout that drops the pin.

**4. Make the canvas cheap and self-limiting.** Pre-render one glow sprite per size class and draw with `lighter`; remove any `shadowBlur`; use integer coordinates. Cap DPR at about 1.5-2 and halve particle count on coarse pointers and small viewports. Add the governor: rolling average of rAF deltas, degrade DPR, then count, then links, with hysteresis. Drive all motion with delta-time so 30 fps Low Power Mode does not slow the story. Pause the loop via IntersectionObserver when the finale is off-screen and on `visibilitychange`. If you draw constellation links, use a grid for neighbour lookup and cap line count. Profile on a mid-range phone before considering WebGL.

**5. Treat mobile viewport units explicitly.** Use `min-height: 100svh` for stage height, size background layers with `lvh` or oversize them to hide toolbar gaps, and resize the canvas on width change only (debounced), never on toolbar-driven height change. Keep native touch scroll, with no scroll smoothing on touch. Replace any mouse parallax with small scroll-driven offsets or remove it.

**6. Keep snap at `proximity` and test it.** Verify Page Down, Space and arrow keys in Safari, Chrome and Firefox with 200-300vh scenes. If keyboard paging fights the snap, remove snap on the tall scenes rather than adding JS.

**7. Protect the depth and the finish of the finale.** Fake depth with size and alpha falloff across a few layers, plus a handful of large soft near-camera discs. Add static grain (tiled noise PNG, low opacity) over the single colour to avoid banding, then check the additive blend against your actual brand colour: on a light or saturated page, switch to tinted normal-alpha sprites. Keep motion reveals 0.6-1.0 s ease-out with scrub smoothed by a small lerp, and keep one primary moving element per beat. Animate only `transform` and `opacity`; apply `will-change` only to the canvas and the one or two pinned panels, and avoid `backdrop-filter` or animated blur over the canvas. Keep flashes well under three per second at scene cuts.

**8. Make the dissolve honest.** Device content that dissolves into stars should use abstract labels (protocol names only) with no real-looking figures, addresses or tickers, and a small "illustrative" caption, consistent with the repo rule that every user-visible number comes from the server ([AGENTS.md](/Users/maksimromanov/code/personal/voidapp-agentsdev/AGENTS.md)). Under reduced motion the frozen frame must carry the same message. The headline stays real `<h1>` text in HTML; avoid per-letter splitting, and use line masks or word spans with a text duplicate if splitting is wanted.

**9. Loading and measurement.** Make headline text or a sized poster the LCP element and start the particle loop after first paint. Reserve the canvas box with `aspect-ratio`, preload and subset the display font with a `size-adjust` fallback, and run any text splitting after `document.fonts.ready`. Measure with Lighthouse and field data; add a Long Animation Frames observer for Chromium only. If you later move to WebGL, handle `webglcontextlost` and `webglcontextrestored` and keep one context per page ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/WebGL_API/WebGL_best_practices)).

## Conclusion

The research mostly validates your stack and shifts the risk elsewhere. Scroll mechanics and rendering are solved problems with strong primary sources; the live uncertainties are legal and perceptual. Apple's "no simulation" clause makes the most visually impressive part of the page, realistic hand-built devices, the one with the weakest footing, while the finale's visual craft rests on design judgement that no source here verifies. Plan to validate the finale by eye on real phones rather than by citation.

The most useful follow-ups are narrow. Get an owner decision on device frames, and if the CSS frames survive, record the reasoning. Measure scene lengths on two or three reference sites and test snap and keyboard paging in three browsers. Re-verify the weak items listed above (Safari 26 GA, Firefox timelines, Low Power Mode detection, WCAG 2.5.8) before they become requirements in a spec.
